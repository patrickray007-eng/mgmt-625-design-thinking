# Claiming Live Performance Royalties (BMI Live)

When you perform **your own written songs** live at a **BMI-licensed venue**, BMI
owes you **performance royalties on those compositions** — separate from and on top
of whatever the venue paid you to play the gig. BMI can't magically know you played
a 40-cap club in Denton, so for anything below the biggest touring acts, **you have
to tell them** by submitting your setlist. That's what **BMI Live** is.

> This is money for the **songwriter/publisher of the works performed**, not a fee
> for performing. If you played a covers-only set of songs you didn't write, BMI
> Live doesn't pay *you* (it'd pay those writers). It pays when **you perform songs
> you wrote**.

---

## Are you eligible?

Yes, if:
- You're a **BMI-affiliated songwriter/composer** (you are), **and**
- **You personally performed** the works (only the performing writer can submit a
  BMI Live setlist), **and**
- The show was at a **BMI-licensed venue** — an ordinary club/bar/restaurant/
  theater covered by a BMI license. There's generally **no headliner requirement**;
  regular gigs count.
- You're **enrolled in Direct Deposit** with BMI (required to participate/get paid).

If BMI can't license the venue/performance, the claim is declined — but the vast
majority of US clubs and bars hold PRO licenses.

---

## How to submit — step by step

1. Log in at **BMI.com** or the **BMI Mobile app**.
2. Open the **BMI Live** section.
3. Enter the **performance date and time**.
4. Enter the **venue** (name + location).
5. Enter your **setlist** — the BMI-registered works you performed. *(Songs must
   be registered at BMI to pay out, so register them first — see below.)*
6. Submit. Payments land on your BMI royalty statement labeled **"BMI Live,"** paid
   **quarterly**.

**Documentation:** BMI Live is largely **self-attested** — you don't upload ticket
stubs or contracts. But **keep your own proof** (setlist, gig confirmation email,
poster, or a photo of the marquee) in case of an audit.

---

## ⚠️ The deadline — don't sit on it

Sources conflict between a **6-month** and a **9-month** submission window. **Treat
it as 6 months from the show date** and submit promptly — ideally within a week
while you still remember the exact setlist. A lapsed window = royalties you can
never claim. *(Exact current window is unverified from a primary source; confirm
on your logged-in BMI Live dashboard.)*

**Anchor dates:** even though those are ASCAP's OnStage quarterly deadlines, they're
a useful mental grid — clear out your live submissions before **Mar 31 / Jun 30 /
Sep 30 / Dec 31** and you'll never miss BMI's window either.

---

## How much is it worth?

- It's derived from the **venue's BMI license fee**, allocated across qualifying
  performances — **not** a published flat rate. Realistically **a few dollars up to
  low tens of dollars per song/performance** at small venues, more at bigger rooms.
- It **adds up across many gigs**, and because you're **self-published, BMI pays you
  both the writer and publisher shares**, so you collect the full amount.
- Exact per-performance figures are **unverified** (BMI doesn't publish a rate).
  Think of it as "found money" for shows you already played — low effort per claim,
  compounding over a gigging year.

## Automating / making it easier

**The honest truth:** BMI has **no public API or bulk CSV uploader** for individual
writers. First-party submission is **manual entry** (web or app). But you can
remove most of the friction:

### Option 1 — Systematize the manual entry (free, do this now)
Keep a running **gig log** so submission is a 5-minute copy-paste, not an
archaeology project. Use `templates/Live-Performance-Log.csv` (created alongside
this file). After every show, add one row: date, venue, city, and the setlist of
**your** songs. Then once a quarter, open BMI Live and enter each logged show.

A good habit: **fill the log the night of the show** (or next morning) from your
actual setlist photo. The hard part of BMI Live isn't the form — it's remembering
months later what you played and where.

### Option 2 — Show-date auto-import tools
- **Muzooka** syncs events from ticketing partners (Ticketmaster, Eventbrite, AXS)
  and streamlines live-royalty setlist submission.
- These pull your **show dates** automatically so you're not reconstructing your
  calendar; you still confirm the setlist.

### Option 3 — Let a publishing admin submit for you
- **Songtrust, Sentric, and CD Baby Pro** will submit setlists to PROs on your
  behalf. **Sentric** integrates with **Bandsintown/Songkick** to pull show dates
  automatically. This is the closest thing to true automation — but it comes with
  the admin's **15–20% cut** on the publishing they collect, and potential overlap
  with Smith Music Group / DistroKid Publishing. Only worth it if you're also using
  the admin for foreign collection and you gig a lot.

**Recommendation for you:** start with **Option 1** (the gig log + a quarterly
calendar reminder). It's free, captures 100% of the value, and takes ~30 min/quarter.
Layer on Option 2/3 only if your show volume makes manual entry genuinely painful.

## Prerequisite: register your songs at BMI first

BMI Live only pays out for **works registered at BMI**. Before (or alongside) your
first submission:
- [ ] Confirm every song you perform live is **registered as a work at BMI** with
  correct writer/publisher splits.
- [ ] Verify you're enrolled in **Direct Deposit**.
- [ ] Grab your **IPI/CAE number** while you're in there — you'll reuse it at The
  MLC and SoundExchange (see `templates/Registration-Checklist.md`).

## BMI vs. ASCAP in 2026 — should you switch? (Short answer: probably stay.)

- You **can't be a writer at both** for the same works — your **IPI ties your
  writer affiliation to one PRO**. You're at BMI.
- **BMI** is free to join, pays a bit faster (~5–6 months after quarter), and you
  already have your IPI and history there.
- **BMI went for-profit** (acquired by New Mountain Capital, closed Feb 2024) and
  reportedly **raised its admin fee take from ~10% to ~15%**. That's the main
  writer-relevant downside; day-to-day claiming is unchanged.
- **ASCAP** is a not-for-profit with a $50 one-time writer fee and its own live
  program (**OnStage**), but switching means changing your IPI affiliation and
  re-registering — friction that rarely pays off unless you're actively unhappy.

**Bottom line:** staying at BMI is the low-friction default. Don't switch reactively
over the fee change alone; switch only if you have a concrete reason.
