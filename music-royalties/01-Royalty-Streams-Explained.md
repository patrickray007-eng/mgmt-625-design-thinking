# Your Royalty Streams, Explained

The single most valuable thing to internalize: **there is no one place that pays
you all your music money.** Your income splits across up to four buckets,
collected by four different organizations, and DistroKid only handles one of them.
Most indie artists lose money simply by not signing up for the other three.

Two rights sit under everything:
- The **composition** ("the song" — the melody + lyrics you *wrote*). Earns
  **performance** and **mechanical** royalties. This is your **songwriter/publisher** hat.
- The **sound recording** / **master** ("the recording" — the specific track you
  *recorded*). Earns **streaming** and **neighboring-rights** royalties. This is
  your **artist/label** hat.

You wear **both hats** (you write *and* record *and* self-release), so you can
collect from every bucket below.

---

## The four buckets

| # | Bucket | Right | Who pays you | Free to collect? | Typical intermediary cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Performance royalties** | Composition | Your PRO — **BMI** | Yes (BMI free for writers) | PRO admin deduction (BMI ~15%); admin ~15% on publisher share only |
| B | **Mechanical royalties** | Composition | **The MLC** (US streaming); admin (foreign) | Yes (The MLC = 0%) | Admin ~15–20% (mostly foreign) |
| C | **Neighboring / digital performance** | Master | **SoundExchange** | Yes (free) | None if you register directly |
| D | **Sync licensing** | Both | You / a sync agent / admin | Free to pursue yourself | Agent/admin ~20–50% (deal-specific, *unverified*) |

Plus the one you already have: **master-side streaming income** (Spotify/Apple
etc.), paid by your **distributor (DistroKid)**, who keeps **0%**.

---

## Bucket A — Performance royalties (BMI)

**What triggers it:** your *song* being performed publicly — streamed, played on
radio/TV, or performed live at a licensed venue.

**Who collects:** your PRO. You're at **BMI**. BMI collects license fees from
venues, streamers, broadcasters, etc., and distributes them to the writers and
publishers of the works performed.

**The writer share vs. publisher share — and the good news for you:** performance
royalties split into a **writer share** and a **publisher share** (BMI expresses
the total as 200%: 100% writer + 100% publisher). A lot of indie writers lose the
publisher half because they never set up a publishing entity. **On BMI, you don't
have to** — for self-published works, **BMI pays both the writer *and* publisher
shares directly to you** (it shows a placeholder "Manuscript" where a publisher
would be). So you are **not** leaving ~50% on the table by lacking a publishing
company. Forming your own BMI publishing company (one-time $175 individual /
$250 LLC) is optional and does **not** increase your total — only do it for
branding, co-pub deals, or separating the accounting.

**How to not leave money here:** make sure **every song is registered at BMI** with
correct splits, and **submit your live shows to BMI Live** (see
`02-Live-Performance-Royalties.md`).

## Bucket B — Mechanical royalties (The MLC)

**What triggers it:** the "reproduction" of your *song* — every stream and download
generates a mechanical royalty on the composition, separate from the performance
royalty and separate from the master-side money DistroKid pays.

**Who collects (US):** **The MLC** (Mechanical Licensing Collective). **Your PRO
does NOT collect these.** DistroKid does NOT collect these (unless you buy its
Publishing add-on). If you've never signed up at The MLC, this money has been
piling up unmatched.

**Cost:** **Free — 0% commission.** The MLC is funded by the DSPs, not by writer fees.

**How to not leave money here:**
- Register at **themlc.com** as a **Self-Administered Songwriter** (legal name,
  stage name, **IPI number** from BMI), then register your catalog.
- Use the **Missing Member / unclaimed royalties lookup** — a large historical
  "black box" of unmatched mechanicals exists, and unclaimed money is eventually
  redistributed to big publishers if you never claim it.
- **Foreign mechanicals** (streams outside the US) are *not* covered by The MLC —
  those need a publishing admin (see below).
- ⚠️ **Do not double-register.** If Smith Music Group, DistroKid Publishing, or a
  service like Songtrust already registers you at The MLC, registering yourself
  again creates conflicts. **Confirm who (if anyone) is already administering your
  mechanicals before you self-register.**

## Bucket C — Neighboring / digital performance royalties (SoundExchange)

**What triggers it:** your *recording* being played on **non-interactive** digital
radio — **SiriusXM, Pandora (radio mode), and webcasters.** This is a master-side
right, completely separate from PROs and mechanicals.

**Who collects:** **SoundExchange.** It pays the recording side on a statutory
split: **50% to the sound-recording owner, 45% to the featured artist, 5% to a
non-featured musicians fund.** Because you **self-release, you are both** the
recording owner and the featured artist — so you collect the lion's share.

**Cost:** **Free** to register (~15 minutes).

**How to not leave money here:** register at **soundexchange.com** as **both**
"performer" and "sound recording copyright owner." Check their unclaimed/back-
catalog lists. Note: this pays you as *artist/label*, not as *writer* — a co-writer
who didn't perform on or own the master gets nothing from this bucket.

---

## Bucket D — Sync licensing

**What triggers it:** placement of your song/recording in film, TV, ads, games, or
trailers. Usually a negotiated up-front fee **plus** downstream performance
royalties (which flow back through BMI).

**Who collects:** you, or a sync agent/publisher with a pitching team (e.g.,
Sentric is noted for placements). Cuts are deal-specific and can be steep
(often ~20–50%, *unverified as a single standard rate*).

**How to not leave money here:** keep clean metadata and registered splits so a
placement can actually be licensed and paid; pitch actively or sign with an admin
that has a real sync team. This is upside, not leakage — it's $0 until you land one.

## Your distributor: DistroKid (master-side streaming)

This is the bucket you already have. Key facts (2026):

- **DistroKid keeps 0% of your streaming royalties** — "keep 100%" is real. Its
  cost to you is the **annual subscription**: Musician **$24.99**, Musician Plus
  **$44.99**, Ultimate **$89.99**. That's a *fixed cost*, so your effective % lost
  to distribution *shrinks* as you earn more.
- **Add-ons that DO take a cut:** **YouTube Content ID (20%)** and **DistroKid
  Publishing Admin (20%)**. These are optional and only worth it if they collect
  money you couldn't get for free yourself.
- **Splits feature (0% cut):** DistroKid **Splits** auto-pays each collaborator
  their % directly into their own DistroKid Bank — no manual re-distribution by
  you, no percentage taken. Collaborators without a paid account can be covered
  for **$10/yr** each. **This is the cleanest way to pay contributors** for
  DistroKid-distributed songs and to make the payout side auditable.
- **Withdrawal fees** (via Tipalti) are per-method: **ACH ~$1.07**, **wire ~$16+**.
  Prefer ACH and withdraw in larger batches. Minimum payout **$6**. Up to **3% FX**
  if paid in a non-USD currency. *(Exact fee figures are lightly unverified —
  confirm in-account.)*
- **Statements** export from Bank → *"See Excruciating Detail"* → Download
  (TSV/CSV). Columns include Reporting Date, Sale Month, Store, Artist, Title,
  ISRC, UPC, Quantity, Team Percentage, and Earnings (USD). The reconciliation
  tool reads these directly.

## Smith Music Group — where it might sit

Research strongly indicates **"Smith Music" = Smith Music Group (SMG)** in the Fort
Worth Stockyards (131 E Exchange Ave; smithmusic.com), a country-focused company
that offers **distribution, royalty collection, catalog management, and publishing
administration (branded "Rick Smith Publishing")**, and runs the *Live at Billy
Bob's Texas* label.

Why this matters for your audit: a single company offering **both distribution
and publishing admin** can be taking a cut on **two** of your buckets at once —
master-side distribution **and** composition-side publishing. If you work with
them, you need to know **exactly which rights they administer and at what
commission**, because:
- Their publishing admin could **overlap or conflict** with DistroKid Publishing,
  Songtrust, or your direct MLC/BMI registrations (double-administration leak).
- Any % they take should show up in your reconciliation as a distribution cost —
  fill it into `config.json` once you confirm it.

**Action:** work through `templates/Smith-Music-Questions.md` with them and record
the answers. Until you confirm a cut, leave their % at 0 in the config so you
don't overstate costs — but flag it as an open question in your audit.

## The "am I leaving money on the table?" cheat sheet

| If you haven't done this… | …you're probably missing |
|---|---|
| Registered at **The MLC** | US streaming **mechanicals** (free money) |
| Registered at **SoundExchange** | SiriusXM/Pandora/webcaster **master** royalties (free money) |
| Registered **every song at BMI** with correct splits | **Performance** royalties on unregistered works |
| Submitted shows to **BMI Live** | **Live performance** royalties (6-month window) |
| Signed a **publishing admin** (if you have real foreign streams) | **Foreign** performance + mechanical royalties (~18–32% of publishing commonly uncollected) |
| Set up **DistroKid Splits** or a payments log | Clean, auditable **contributor payouts** (and dispute protection) |

**Sources** for all figures above are cited in the research that generated this
toolkit; the load-bearing ones: BMI writer/publisher split & self-publish handling
(bmi.com, aristake.com), The MLC (themlc.com), SoundExchange split
(soundexchange.com), DistroKid fees & Splits (distrokid.com support + 2026 reviews),
Smith Music Group (smithmusic.com, Fort Worth Stockyards listing). Figures flagged
*unverified* could not be confirmed to a primary source and should be checked
in-account.
